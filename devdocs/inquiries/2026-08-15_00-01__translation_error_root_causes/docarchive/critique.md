## Phase 1 — Fitness Landscape

**Viable region.** Claims that (a) are grounded in artifacts a third party can open, (b) survive the recursion objection (the remedy does not require the noticing it exists to supply), and (c) carry a confidence their stated ground supports.

**Dead region.** Any claim whose stated ground is demonstrably not met by its own cited evidence — this is fatal regardless of whether the claim happens to be true, because the finding's entire authority rests on being checkable. A true claim on a false ground is precisely the "PASS with no check fired" shape under diagnosis.

**Boundary region.** Claims that are directionally right but whose supporting evidence is thinner or more confounded than stated; and remedies that work under a restriction the finding does not state.

**Unexplored region (found).** Nothing in the candidate set addresses **cross-document persistence** — every remedy is per-run or per-document, while the corpus is a multi-volume work with recurring honorifics, recurring technical vocabulary, and a recurring policy set. Flagged at C13.

## Phase 2–3 — Adversarial Evaluation and Verdicts

### C1 — The five-property frame (P2)

**Prosecution.** Any decomposition into N properties, applied to a fix that touched one of them, produces the rhetorical effect "it only fixed one!" Try a rival: *(who checks / what they check / how carefully / how often / with what authority)*. July's fix touched one of those too. The frame's persuasiveness is decomposition-generic, not evidence-specific.

**Defense.** The rival decomposition does not sort the errors. Under the rival, "how carefully" absorbs everything and predicts nothing. The five-property frame makes a specific, checkable assignment — and more importantly it **predicts a check nobody has run**: P3's footnote-density example classifies as a scope gap without any error prompting it. A frame that generates unprompted predictions is not merely a retrospective story.

**Collision.** The defense holds on the generative point. The prosecution succeeds on a narrower target: the frame's *persuasive force* is partly decomposition-generic and the finding presents it as though the force came from the evidence.

**Verdict: SURVIVE** — with two caveats: the count is unstable (see C2/D2 below) and the finding should state that a decomposition-shaped explanation is cheap, and that what distinguishes this one is that it sorts and predicts.

*Dimension positions:* D1 pass · D2 **caveat** (naming defect, see C2) · D4 caveat (falsifiability arrives only at P11's prediction) · D7 pass.

### C2 — The HIGH-confidence ground: "each axis shown varying while others are held fixed, by a specific pair" (P2)

This is the single most load-bearing methodological claim in the finding. It gets full substance-axis prosecution: each pair is tested by applying the pair's own logic literally.

**Pair 1 — vantage vs instrument.** *A3 (sentence boundaries; source+target; script) vs A1 (content dropped; source+target; prose).* Same vantage ✓, different instrument ✓ — **but the `what` is not held fixed.** A3's subject is a countable structural property; A1's is an open-ended semantic one. A3 was scriptable *because* its `what` is enumerable. So the pair varies two properties, and the observed outcome difference is at least as well explained by "one question is closed and the other is open" as by "one had an instrument." **The pair does not demonstrate independence.**

**Pair 2 — instrument vs position.** *The re-scans (prose instrument, separate-call position, 17 found) vs the in-turn Pass 4 (prose instrument, prose position, 0 found).* This is the finding's strongest-looking pair, and it is **the most badly confounded.** The user's first re-scan request was not "check again." It was:

> *"…make sure it followed SKILL/references/config/schemas.py and our config selections… and make sure you first read the original and understand **sentence by sentence and paragraph by paragraph** and then check if english translation has some weird translations…"*

That request supplies **three** things: a fresh turn (**position**), an explicit direction to check against `schemas.py` and the config (**scope** — the very thing Pass 4 excludes by definition), and an explicit granularity, sentence-by-sentence and paragraph-by-paragraph (**an instrument** — a forced enumeration, in plain language). The instrument was *not* held fixed across the pair. The user supplied one.

**Pair 3 — vantage vs position.** *The re-scans were at the strong position with the source present and found zero of the two target-only errors.* Directionally clean — **but "zero of two" is an overcount.** The pronoun error did not exist during the first re-scan; it was introduced by a later tightening edit. The honest statement is *zero of the one target-only error that existed throughout.* n = 1, not 2.

**Pair 4 — scope vs everything.** *The policy check has no vantage, instrument, position or timing problem; it has no definition.* This is **not a variation pair at all.** A check that does not exist has no values on any axis. It demonstrates that scope can fail alone; it does not demonstrate scope varying while others are held fixed.

**Pair 5 — time vs everything.** *The regression passed every check that ran; the check ran before the edit.* Same check, same what, same vantage, same instrument, same position; only timing differs. **Clean.** The only pair that meets the stated criterion.

**Prosecution's summary:** the stated ground is met by **one of five** pairs. Three are confounded and one is not a pair.

**Defense.** The properties are still separable — the frame does not actually need variation pairs. Scope's independence follows from the *logical* fact that a check must be defined before it can be positioned. Vantage's independence follows from Pair 3 even at n=1, plus the direct evidence that two dedicated re-scans found none of it. The claim is true; the advertised proof is the wrong proof.

**Collision.** The defense saves the *frame* (C1) and loses the *ground*. And the ground's failure matters more than usual here, because the finding's whole authority rests on being checkable by a reader who disagrees. A reader who checks the pairs finds three that do not hold — inside a document arguing that unchecked assertions are the problem.

**Verdict: KILL** — the ground-claim specifically, not the frame.

**Seed extracted.** *"Demonstrated by variation pairs" dies because scope admits no variation pair and two others are confounded. What if the correct ground is independent failure rather than independent variation?* Restated: **each property is shown to be able to fail while the others are satisfied.** That is true of all five, requires no controlled pair, and is exactly what the evidence supports. Confidence should then be stated as **HIGH on separability, MED on the pair-based demonstration** — and Pair 2's confound should be reported, not buried, because it turns out to carry the finding's best evidence (see C7).

Would it have predicted, in July, that a fix to pass-ordering would leave dropped words and wrong word-senses? Only in the vaguest sense — it predicts "something not-ordering recurs," which is nearly unfalsifiable.

The frame is therefore not unfalsifiable — but the falsifiable content lives in P11, not in P7.

**Collision.** The explanation is retrospective fitting *plus* one forward commitment. Presented as it stands, the retrospective half carries rhetorical weight it has not earned, and the forward half is buried in a numbered action item.

*What "right" looks like on D4:* the reader should be able to see what would falsify the account before being asked to accept it.

### C4 — The gap partition and its counts (P5: 10 / 6 / 1 / 1 / 1)

**Prosecution (the assignment rule).** "Assign by the cheapest check that would have caught it" is not a principle — it is a convenience, and it manufactures the instrument/position split. A1 asks *"was any source content dropped?"* Read broadly, that covers losing `dirilmek`'s "come back to life" sense — content was dropped from inside the word. Read narrowly, it covers only wholly-absent tokens. **The 10/6 split is a function of how generously A1 is read, and nothing in the finding fixes the reading.** Read A1 broadly and almost everything is position; read it narrowly and almost everything is instrument.

**Prosecution (the premise).** Per the frame-premise test, three of the ten instrument-kind items are exactly the ones P13 admits are **undecidable** as errors without a config-comparison check. The count leans on items whose membership in the error set the finding itself says cannot currently be determined.

**Defense.** A principled line is available, and the finding is one sentence away from it: **absence is detectable by alignment; wrongness inside an alignment is not.** `içyüzü` is absent — a reader comparing texts sees a source word with no counterpart. `dirilmek`'s wrong sense is present-but-wrong — the counterpart exists and the error is inside it. That is a real, statable distinction that does not depend on generous reading.

**Collision.** The defense rescues the *categories* and destroys the *counts*. Applying the alignment rule moves at least one item: `zarurî` is present-but-weakened, i.e. wrongness inside an alignment → instrument, not position. The split becomes 11/5. Under a slightly different but equally defensible reading it moves again.

**Verdict: REFINE.**
*What must change:* (a) replace "cheapest check" with the alignment principle, stated explicitly; (b) **stop putting weight on the counts.** P1 currently leads with "Ten happened because… Six happened because…" — those numbers are unstable under a rule the finding has not yet fixed, and P12's proportions-are-unusable caveat does not reach P1. The kinds are the payload; the counts should be given as *approximate* and explicitly marked unstable, or dropped from P1 entirely; (c) mark the three undecidable items in P5 itself rather than only in P13.

### C5 — The substitution test and its 4/4 result (P9)

**Prosecution.** A test every candidate passes is not visibly a test. The finding never exhibits a rejection, so a reader cannot tell whether the test discriminates or merely licenses.

**Prosecution (deeper).** Even granting that it discriminates, the test screens for *arrangement-vs-agent* — it does not screen for *load-bearing-ness*. "A producer cannot check their own work" passes the test, is true, and is also the canonical shape of a self-serving explanation. Passing is necessary, not sufficient.

**Defense.** The test does reject. Constructed counter-example: *"A model cannot tell when it is confabulating a source meaning it does not actually know."* Substituted: a human translator who does not know a word experiences not-knowing and looks it up or flags it; the not-knowing is available to them in a way it is not straightforwardly available to a model. **That claim fails the test and is correctly excluded** as agent-property rather than arrangement-property. The instrument works.

**Collision.** The test is sound and under-exhibited. Its stated scope is also slightly overclaimed.

**Verdict: REFINE.**
*What must change:* include the failing example in P9 — one line — so the reader sees the test reject something. And state the limit: passing means the claim is about the arrangement, not that the claim is load-bearing for this failure.

### C6 — The vantage impossibility argument (P9, P10-R4)

**Prosecution (a) — "no operation removes information from a context" does too much work.** True, and it proves less than it is used for. The claim actually needed is not *information cannot be removed* but *the judgment is contaminated by the information*. Those come apart. A model holding the source could be asked for a **mechanical** output that does not depend on the contaminated judgment: *"list every referring expression and the nearest preceding noun phrase, without evaluating resolvability."* That list is producible identically with or without the source. Only the *evaluative* half — "is this antecedent the right one / is it recoverable?" — requires the source's absence.

**So the vantage is not monolithic.** It decomposes into a mechanical enumeration (in-call, free) and an evaluative judgment (separate call). The finding treats it as all-or-nothing and thereby makes the remedy look more architecturally demanding than it is.

**Prosecution (b) — "cheapest call in the method."** Cheapest in tokens. Not cheapest in integration: something must route the question-list back into a source-present context and act on it. That workflow cost is unpriced.

**Prosecution (c) — the question-list construction relocates invention rather than preventing it.** The source-blind reader cannot write into the text, granted. But it can generate **false-positive questions** — flagging resolvable references as unresolvable — and each such question prompts an edit in the source-present context. And the finding's own evidence shows what quality-motivated edits do: **error 19 was a tightening edit.** So R4 manufactures precisely the class of edit-pressure that produced the regression. **R4 is unsafe without R3.** The finding presents the six remedies as complements with "almost no overlap" and misses a hard dependency between two of them.

**Defense.** The vantage claim itself stands: two dedicated re-scans under explicit instruction found seventeen source-comparison errors and none of the target-only one. Whatever the mechanism, the in-call version demonstrably did not work.

**Collision.** The conclusion survives; three of its supporting claims are overstated, and one omission (R4→R3) is a real design defect.

**Verdict: REFINE.**
*What must change:* (a) restate the impossibility as *judgment contamination*, and split R4 into a mechanical in-call enumeration plus an evaluative separate call — this makes it cheaper and more robust, not less; (b) price the routing cost; (c) state R4's dependency on R3 explicitly, and re-order P11 so R3 ships **before or with** R4, never after.

### C7 — P8's division-of-labour claim and the X01 dismissal

**Prosecution (register).** Test by substitution as the piece requires: replace the user with a junior colleague, a second model, a client. *"A necessary position was staffed by accident and never assigned"* stays structural. **Passes.** But P1's phrasing does not: *"if you hadn't asked twice, the translation would have shipped with nineteen errors under a verdict of PASS"* has the **user as the grammatical subject of a counterfactual about failure.** It is true and load-bearing and one reading away from "you should have asked more." D5 fails on that sentence specifically.

**Prosecution (X01).** *"It measures the working pair's performance, not the method's"* is a convenient distinction. Every method is executed by someone; a method that assumes an operator is not thereby defective. Plenty of processes assume one.

**Prosecution (the confound — the finding's own best evidence, mis-attributed).** Per C2/Pair 2: the user's re-scan request supplied a **position**, a **scope**, and an **instrument**. P8 attributes the 17 catches to the separateness of the turn alone. That attribution is not supported by the evidence — the same request also told the model *what* to check against (`schemas.py` and the config, which Pass 4 excludes) and *at what granularity* (sentence by sentence, paragraph by paragraph, which is a forced enumeration).

**Defense.** The X01 objection is answerable, but not the way P8 answers it. The defect is not "the method caught zero" — it is that **the method reported complete while depending on a step it does not contain and does not name.** Pass 4 emitted PASS. A process that reports completion while silently depending on an unspecified operator action is misreporting, and that is a defect regardless of who executes it.

**And the confound is a gift.** Correctly read, the user's request is the finding's **strongest single piece of evidence for the five-property frame**: without any theory, a person supplied fixes for three of the five properties in one sentence, and the error yield jumped from zero to ten. That is far better than "they created a separate call."

**Collision.** P8's conclusion holds; its attribution is wrong and under-claims; its framing has one sentence that misplaces the subject.

**Verdict: REFINE — highest-value refinement in this critique.**
*What must change:* (a) re-attribute — the user supplied position **and** scope **and** instrument, quote the request, and use it as direct evidence for the frame; (b) re-frame the X01 answer around *reported-complete-while-depending-on-an-uncontained-step*; (c) rewrite P1's counterfactual so the method is the subject: *"the method had no step that would have found them; the only thing that did was a request it does not know it needs."*

### C8 — Pointability, and the third instance (P12)

**Prosecution (pointability).** It conflates *checkability* with *correctness*. A claim can be pointable and wrong — the file says X, but X does not entail the claim. Pointability licenses HIGH confidence in a **premise**, not in an **inference from it**. And the finding applies it past that line: "vantage ⊥ position" is an inference from pairs (three of which are confounded), not a pointable fact, yet it sits inside the HIGH bucket.

**Prosecution (the third instance).** `structural_check.sh` is a **different phenomenon wearing the same clothes.** The traverse spec explicitly anticipates its absence and specifies the fallback — *"If `tools/structural_check.sh` is unavailable, manually check the discipline's required structure and record the result."* That is a sanctioned degradation path, not a check that was never instrumented. Pass 4 never contemplated an instrument at all. Calling this a third instance of the same pattern inflates n.

**Defense.** Pointability is a real criterion for *transferability*, which is what a finding needs in order to be checkable by someone who was not in the room — and the finding says so. The third instance does support something: the *outcome* is identical (prose self-assessment by the producing context, recorded as passed), which is direct evidence for the **instrument-axis** claim specifically.

**Collision.** Both survive in narrower form than claimed.

**Verdict: REFINE.**
*What must change:* (a) split the confidence one level finer — HIGH on pointable premises, MED on inferences drawn from them, and move "vantage ⊥ position" into the MED bucket; (b) state precisely what the third instance supports: **the instrument-axis claim, at n=3**, not the five-property structure at n=3 — and note the disanalogy (a sanctioned fallback is not an unconsidered absence) rather than letting the instance do more work than it can.

### C9 — R1 as prevention (P10) · *the objection the finding does not face*

**Prosecution.** R1 says: before drafting, enumerate *"every polysemous term with its candidate senses."* **Who decides which terms are polysemous?** The same context, using the same noticing that failed. If the model does not notice `bitiyor` is polysemous, it will not enumerate it, and R1 produces a list that omits exactly the words that go on to cause errors. **The noticing problem recurses one step back, and the finding's leading remedy is answering it with the thing that failed.** This is the strongest objection available to the whole account, and P10 does not engage it.

**Defense — and it has to be structural, not hopeful.** The objection is fatal to *judgment-keyed* enumerations and harmless to *form-keyed* ones. Compare:

- *"List every polysemous word"* — requires the noticing. **Dies.**
- *"List every Arabic/Ottoman-loan word in this passage and give each one's senses"* — requires only a morphological filter. The class is identifiable **by form**, not by whether ambiguity was noticed. Then sense-listing runs over the whole set, exhaustively rather than selectively. **Survives.**
- *"List every word appearing more than once, with counts"* — mechanical. **Survives.**
- *"List every technical term the source names in its own vocabulary"* — the source literally contains the string `istifham-ı inkârî`. **Survives.**
- *"List every honorific form and every transliterated string"* — form-detectable. **Survives.**

**Collision.** R1 as written is half-vulnerable — it mixes a judgment-keyed enumeration with form-keyed ones and presents them as one thing. Corrected, it survives cleanly.

**Verdict: REFINE.**
*What must change:* R1's enumerations must be **keyed to form-detectable classes**, never to semantic judgments. Rewrite the spec so no line reads "list every X that is [semantic property]"; every line must read "list every X of [detectable form], then state its [semantic property]." State the principle explicitly, because it is the thing that answers the recursion objection — and the objection should be raised in the text rather than left for a reader to find.

### C10 — The remedy set: availability, and the no-new-prose constraint test (P10, P11)

**Prosecution.** The constraint test — *add "no new prose may be added to any runtime file" and see what survives* — is a good test that is **passed too easily**. R3 is claimed to survive "as an invalidation rule attached to an artifact," but **no artifact is specified for it.** As written, R3 is a sentence: *"any edit made after verification re-runs the checks whose span it touched."* Put that sentence in `translation_method.md` and it is prose in a runtime file at the weakest gradient position — **the fourth instance of the pattern, inside the remedy set of the document diagnosing it.** D6 fails.

**Defense.** R3 is repairable into the artifact form the test assumed: verification emits a record naming the draft-state it verified (a hash, a line-count, a quoted span). An edit changes the state. The record is then *visibly* stale rather than merely notionally invalid. That is a carrier, and it costs almost nothing.

**Collision.** The claim "five of six are available now" survives. The claim that the set passes the no-new-prose test does **not** survive for R3 as specified.

**Verdict: REFINE.**
*What must change:* give R3 a carrier — a verification record that names what it verified — or reclassify it honestly as a prose nudge and say so. Re-run the constraint test afterwards and report the result rather than asserting it.

Examining the survivors and refinements together, one architecture emerges that no individual piece names — and it is stronger than the five-property frame as an actionable takeaway.
