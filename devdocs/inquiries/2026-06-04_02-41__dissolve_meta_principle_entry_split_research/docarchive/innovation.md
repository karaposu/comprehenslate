### Mechanism 3 — Inversion (Framer)

**Level 1.** "We're recommending dissolution" → "we're recommending NOT continuing the prior path." Component-level.

**Level 2 (depth-check).** "The meta-principle is an addition to the framework" → "the meta-principle is a LEGACY ARTIFACT the framework has outgrown." System-level: the meta-principle predates the per-entry-reasoning that Tier 1/2 entries now embody; the principle was useful when the entries didn't have reasoning, but they do now.

**Level 3 (root).** "We're proposing a structural change" → "we're proposing structural SIMPLIFICATION — the change IS the removal of a layer that wasn't earning its complexity." Root-level: dissolution is subtractive, not additive.

**Multi-axis.**
- *Existence-axis:* what if ZERO entries actually need the meta-principle to make sense? Per surfacing B-7/B-8, Tier 1/2 entries don't; Tier 3 entries' meta-principle dependence is exactly what's failing. Empirical answer: very few or zero entries genuinely need it.
- *Identity-axis:* what is harmony_layer.md FUNDAMENTALLY? A doc of meaning-priority decisions for translation. Dissolved form makes the decisions directly visible per entry; meta-principle abstracted them away.

### Mechanism 4 — Constraint Manipulation (Framer)

- *Generic ADD:* "every Tier 3 entry MUST be converted to D2 form" — non-negotiable for dissolution to address the contradiction pattern.
- *Focused ADD:* "the meta-principle must be MOVED OR REMOVED; cannot remain as a procedural artifact in the doc."
- *Contrarian ADD:* "the worked examples must show BEFORE-AFTER side-by-side, not just AFTER" — forces the deliverable to visualize the change.

**REMOVE-direction.**
