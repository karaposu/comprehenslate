##### **P3.1 — `very_basic`**
- Reader: young child / brand-new L2 learner
- Idiom-frequency: 0 (treats all figurative literally)
- Register: NONE
- Handling: AGGRESSIVE PARAPHRASE
- Examples: none positive; negatives = ANY idiom ("piece of cake")

##### **P3.2 — `daily`**
- Reader: functional adult (backpacker / new immigrant)
- Idiom-frequency: top ~30 transparent + few high-frequency opaque
- Register: casual + most-common conversational; excludes literary, archaic, biblical
- Handling: PARAPHRASE most; FAMILIAR-EQUIVALENT top-30
- Examples positive: "piece of cake", "easy as 1-2-3", "rain or shine", "out of the blue", "break the ice", "under the weather", "once in a blue moon"
- Examples negative: "kick the bucket" (conversational), "cast aspersions" (advanced)

##### **P3.3 — `conversational`**
- Reader: average newspaper-reading educated adult
- Idiom-frequency: top ~200–500 common idioms incl. opaque
- Register: casual + conversational-educated + journalistic
- Handling: FAMILIAR-EQUIVALENT for common opaque; KEEP transparent; PARAPHRASE rare/literary-archaic
- Examples positive: "kick the bucket" (user's anchor), "spill the beans", "hit the nail on the head", "let the cat out of the bag", "burn the midnight oil", "bite the bullet", "the ball is in your court", "go the extra mile"
- Examples negative: "cast aspersions" (advanced), "give up the ghost" (native)

##### **P3.4 — `advanced`**
- Reader: university-educated / skilled non-native reading widely
- Idiom-frequency: top ~1000–2000 incl. academic/literary
- Register: casual + conversational + journalistic + academic + general literary
- Handling: KEEP most; PARAPHRASE / GLOSS only archaic / Biblical; FAMILIAR-EQUIVALENT for borderline
- Examples positive: "cast aspersions", "tilt at windmills", "throw down the gauntlet", "rise to the occasion", "Pyrrhic victory" (DUAL-MEMBERSHIP — cross-ref P6), "play one's cards close to the chest", "lay an egg" (theater)
- Examples negative (above advanced): A1.native ("give up the ghost"; archaic Biblical); A2 specialist (legal "with all deliberate speed"; financial "below the line")
- Cross-ref P5 + P6

##### **P3.5 — `native`**
- Reader: educated native reading broadly incl. literary-extreme
- Idiom-frequency: all general idioms incl. archaic/Biblical/Shakespeare-derived
- Register: ALL general including archaic, biblical, Shakespeare-derived
- Handling: KEEP all general; only A2 specialist gets glossed/footnoted
- Examples positive: "give up the ghost", "the patience of Job", "by the skin of my teeth", "a thorn in my side", "cast pearls before swine", "method in his madness", "more in sorrow than in anger", "the slings and arrows of outrageous fortune", "Crossing the Rubicon" (DUAL-MEMBERSHIP — cross-ref P6), "Trojan horse" (DUAL-MEMBERSHIP — cross-ref P6)
- Examples negative (A2 specialist): legal "with all deliberate speed"; financial "below the line"; sports-specialist "Hail Mary pass"; medical specialist idioms
- Cross-ref P5 + P6

#### **P4 — Adjacent-level boundary specs**

Sub-decomposes into P4.1–P4.4; each verifies distinguishing principle + 3–5 idiom-pair examples.

##### **P4.1 — `very_basic` ↔ `daily`**
- Principle: shift from zero idiom recognition to top-30 transparent idioms
- Pairs (low-side literal → high-side recognized): "very easy" → "piece of cake"; "very rarely" → "once in a blue moon"

##### **P4.2 — `daily` ↔ `conversational`**
- Principle: shift from universally-transparent only to common OPAQUE idioms entering
- Pairs: "very easy" (still daily) ↔ "kick the bucket" (conversational — opaque, requires recognition)
- Other pairs: "made it" ↔ "made the cut"; "told a secret" ↔ "spilled the beans"

##### **P4.3 — `conversational` ↔ `advanced`**
- Principle: shift from common opaque to LESS COMMON LITERARY/ACADEMIC idioms
- Pairs: "criticized" ↔ "cast aspersions"; "attempted impossibly" ↔ "tilted at windmills"; "challenged" ↔ "threw down the gauntlet"

##### **P4.4 — `advanced` ↔ `native`**
- Principle: shift from modern academic-literary to ARCHAIC / BIBLICAL / SHAKESPEARE-derived
- Pairs: "died" ↔ "gave up the ghost"; "showed great patience" ↔ "had the patience of Job"; "barely succeeded" ↔ "by the skin of my teeth"; "wasted effort on those who won't appreciate" ↔ "cast pearls before swine"
