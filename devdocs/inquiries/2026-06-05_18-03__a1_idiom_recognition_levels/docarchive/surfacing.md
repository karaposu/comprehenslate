### Frontier flags

1. **Template-adaptation question** — for idioms, frequency-tier applies directly (idioms have frequency distributions like words). Register-tier applies. The substitution-test almost certainly becomes idiom-handling-test with named actions. So the adaptation is LIGHTER than for syntax. Sensemaking should confirm the per-component decisions.

2. **Compositionality as a 5th component?** — idiom compositionality (transparent vs opaque) is a strong cognitive variable. Should it be a 5th template component, or absorbed into the frequency-tier? Decision likely: absorb into the per-level prose; not its own component.

3. **Cross-sub-field boundary: idiom-recognition vs cultural-reference-recognition.** Some expressions are both ("Achilles' heel" — idiom AND cultural ref). Sensemaking should adjudicate: do they count under BOTH sub-fields independently (a reader must recognize both the idiomatic meaning AND the cultural reference)? Or does one subsume the other? Likely: independently — the idiom-recognition aspect is "Achilles' heel = vulnerability point"; the cultural-reference aspect is "Achilles from Greek mythology." Both must be settled in their respective sub-fields.

4. **A1↔A2 boundary for idioms.** General idioms (everyday → archaic) are A1. Domain-specialist idioms requiring field training (financial "below the line"; legal "with all deliberate speed") are A2. Borderline cases: domain-derived idioms now in general use ("moving the goalposts"; "in the trenches"). Sensemaking should specify the test ("requires domain training to know the figurative meaning").

5. **Proverbs vs idioms.** Proverbs ("a stitch in time saves nine"; "the early bird catches the worm") are sentence-length and often culturally-anchored. Are they a sub-category of idioms? Or separate? Sensemaking should decide whether the level spec covers proverbs (likely yes, with proverbs concentrated at advanced/native levels).

6. **Phrasal verbs as quasi-idioms.** "Look up", "give in", "carry on", "make out" — some are transparent (look up = direct upward), some idiomatic (give in = surrender). Sensemaking should locate them — likely many sit at daily / conversational because they're high-frequency and learned with vocabulary.

7. **Dead metaphors.** "Understand" / "comprehend" / "grasp" (in cognitive sense) — these are metaphorical etymologically but now LITERAL in usage. They do NOT belong in the idiom-recognition gradient because no reader processes them as figurative. Flag for sensemaking to exclude from the spec.

8. **Universal vs language-specific idioms.** Some metaphorical patterns are cross-linguistically universal ("flooding" emotion); most idioms are language-specific. The language-agnosticism constraint applies to the CONCEPT (idiom-frequency × register gradient); specific idioms are per-language.

9. **Migration mapping** — likely parallel to vocabulary-breadth and syntactic-processing-capacity ('s migrations): `late_learner_simple → daily`, `late_learner → conversational`, `native → native`; new positions `very_basic` and `advanced`. Confirm.

10. **The user's "backpacker won't understand idioms" framing.** Per D4, the user originally framed backpacker-level readers as NOT recognizing idioms. This locates the daily-level reader as having LOW (not zero) idiom recognition. Sensemaking should reconcile: very_basic = zero idiom recognition; daily = recognizes only the top-50 universally-transparent idioms ("piece of cake"); conversational = recognizes common opaque (user's "kick the bucket"). The backpacker framing locates between very_basic and daily.
