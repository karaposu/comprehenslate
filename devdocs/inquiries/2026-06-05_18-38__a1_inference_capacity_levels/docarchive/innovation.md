### Cluster CC-J — Template-adaptation rationale (MEDIUM-heaviness)

**Comparison to siblings:**

- **Idiom-recognition (LIGHT):** only substitution-test replaced (idiom-handling test); frequency-tier and register-tier kept with light prefix-rename. Idioms behave like vocabulary in frequency-and-register distributions.

- **Syntactic-processing-capacity (HEAVY):** frequency-tier replaced entirely (structural-complexity tier); register-tier reframed substantially (register/genre-tier with new semantics); substitution-test replaced (restructuring-test). Sentences don't follow Zipfian distributions and syntactic register has its own dynamics.

- **Inference-capacity (MEDIUM):** three components substantively adapted (frequency-tier → inference-load tier with 6 sub-measures; register-tier → register/genre-tier; substitution-test → gap-filling test with 6 actions). Underlying tier+handling pattern preserved. Inferences are PROCESSES (not items like vocabulary or idioms); cognitive load is the right axis (not corpus frequency). The adaptation parallels syntactic-processing-capacity's structure but with less architectural restructuring.

**Why MEDIUM and not HEAVY:** the OPERATIONAL structure parallels: inference-load tier is an umbrella with sub-measures (like structural-complexity tier); gap-filling test names runtime actions (like restructuring-test). The architectural pattern is preserved.

**Why MEDIUM and not LIGHT:** three components substantively adapted (vs idiom's one); frequency-tier doesn't apply to processes; register-tier needed reframing for inference-demand variation by genre; substitution-test → 6-action gap-filling.

**The principle remains: adapt where needed; preserve where it fits.** Inferences are closer to syntax (process-based) than to vocabulary/idioms (item-based), so the adaptation is heavier; but the operational tier+handling pattern carries over from syntactic-processing-capacity.

**Disposition: ACTIONABLE.**
