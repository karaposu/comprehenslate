## (c) Candidate Verdicts (prosecution · defense · collision)

### CAND-1 — the two-part selection criterion (as closure-rationale) · **SURVIVE (with caveat)**
- **Prosecution:** the criterion doesn't mechanically sort — it needs judgment and can misfire (see CAND-3 word-sense misfiling); "entailment" overstates what a two-question rule delivers.
- **Defense:** two questions a SKILL-author can actually apply; it *is* the membership rule that keeps the list closed and prevents the check-agenda-too-large failure (DF5).
- **Collision:** the criterion earns its keep as **curation guidance**, not as a mechanical partition. **Caveat:** state it as "criterion-**guided**, judgment-applied," not "scope falls out as entailment."

### CAND-2 — the two-column Pass-3 spine checklist · **REFINE**
- **Prosecution (D3, the sharpest landed hit):** the column assignment has at least one **misfiled member**. The innovation put **"word-sense right?"** in the *config-derived* column — but by the project's own polysemy principle (the correct sense is fixed by the **local source construction** — genitive/plausibility — **not** by reader level A1), word-sense correctness is **config-INDEPENDENT**. So the clean "config-derived = word-sense / too-hard-word / naturalness" list is wrong: *too-hard-word* and *naturalness* are config-derived (keyed to A1), but *word-sense* is not. The two-column split is real, but membership was partly assigned by **association** ("it's about word choice") rather than by the criterion itself.
- **Defense:** the misfiling is a data-error, not a frame-error — and it actually **strengthens** the config-dependence axis: applying the criterion *carefully* re-sorts members, which is exactly what an axis worth having should do.
- **Collision:** **REFINE** — keep the two-column architecture; **audit every member against the criterion** rather than by topic; move word-sense-correctness to the config-independent side (it is source-fixed). *Constructive:* the criterion works; the innovation's demo table under-applied it.

### CAND-2b — NEW FINDING: the missing **third axis (mechanical-checkability)** · **REFINE the spine model**
- **Prosecution:** the spine's members are **not uniform in reliability**. omission-diff / no-add / no-merge-split are **mechanically checkable** (enumerate source clauses, check presence/boundaries) → rubber-stamp-**resistant**. escalation / ring / havuz are **judgment checks** → rubber-stamp-**prone** (the same fluency-biased model can glance and declare "preserved"). The two-axis model (must-wait ∧ config-independent) treats them as one cell, but the **rubber-stamp-one-level-up objection** (job 1c) bites the soft members and *not* the mechanical ones.
- **Defense:** the mechanical core is precisely why prior `2026-07-10_23-03` isolated "the **mechanical** omission-diff" — reliability was always the implicit selector; the spine just needs to name it.
- **Collision:** **REFINE** — the genuinely-valuable core = **must-wait ∧ config-independent ∧ mechanically-checkable**; the structural-fidelity checks are must-wait ∧ config-independent but *not* mechanical → a **soft rim**, useful but rubber-stamp-prone, to be ordered after the mechanical core. This is the critique's substantive addition (parallel to Inquiry-2's granularity correction): a **third filter** the innovation's clean intersection-cell omitted.

### CAND-3 — scope-by-entailment · **REFINE → "criterion-guided, not entailed"**

### CAND-5 — the two-axis reframe (firing-time=placement / config-independence=value) · **SURVIVE (REFINE phrasing)**
