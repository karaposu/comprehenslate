## Phase 3 — Ambiguity Collapse

### Ambiguity 1 — How to replace frequency-tier in the template?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** Keep "frequency-tier" labelled but redefine as "structural-frequency tier" — frequency of similar structures in target-language corpora.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** Structural-frequency conflates two distinct things — the RELATIVE rarity of a construction in corpora AND the cognitive difficulty of parsing it. A double center-embedding is RARE AND difficult; a long coordinated sentence is COMMON but EASY. For vocabulary, frequency and difficulty correlate strongly (rare words are harder); for syntax, the correlation is much weaker. The correct analogue is COGNITIVE DIFFICULTY, not corpus frequency.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Resolution:** Replace "frequency-tier" with **structural-complexity tier** — an umbrella for sentence-length + embedding-depth + suspension-load + word-order canonicality.

**Fixed:** Template's second component is structural-complexity tier with 4 sub-measures.

### Ambiguity 2 — Keep register-tier component for syntax?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** Drop register-tier; syntactic complexity is the primary axis; genre/register is downstream and partially correlated.

**Why partially holds, partially fails:** Register and syntactic complexity correlate (academic register is hypotactic and dense; casual register is paratactic and simple), but genres at SIMILAR complexity differ in syntactic PROFILE (academic at conversational complexity uses different patterns than literary fiction at conversational complexity). The genre anchor is operationally useful for the user to identify their target reader.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Resolution:** Keep but reframe as **register/genre-tier** — which writing genres the reader can parse (children's signs / practical guides / journalism / academic / literary-extreme).

**Fixed:** Template's third component is register/genre-tier.

### Ambiguity 4 — Where does center-embedding 2-deep sit on the 5-level scale?

**Confidence:** HIGH.

### SV6 — Stabilized Model

The 5 syntactic-processing-capacity levels:

```
LEVEL 1 — very_basic
  Reader profile: young child / brand-new L2 learner
  Structural-complexity tier:
    - Sentence-length (English): ≤6 words/sentence
    - Embedding depth: 0 (no embedding)
    - Suspension load: 0
    - Word order: canonical SVO only
  Register/genre-tier: only children's signs / Dr. Seuss-style early-reader prose;
                       excludes coordination beyond minimal, subordination, nominalization, marked word orders
  Restructuring-test sketch: aggressive — SPLIT (break any compound sentence),
                              UNEMBED (pull any embedded clause out), LINEARIZE,
                              ADD-CONNECTIVES, REDUCE-NOMINALIZATIONS

LEVEL 2 — daily
  Reader profile: functional adult (backpacker / new immigrant / functional L2 speaker)
  Structural-complexity tier:
    - Sentence-length (English): ≤15 words/sentence
    - Embedding depth: ≤1 (one simple embedded clause max)
    - Suspension load: ≤1 (short embedded element delaying main verb)
    - Word order: canonical SVO; minimal marked orders
  Register/genre-tier: practical guides, simple news headlines, casual instructional prose;
                       excludes multi-clause subordination, parentheticals, marked word orders
  Restructuring-test sketch: moderate — SPLIT sentences > 15 words,
                              ADD-CONNECTIVES (replace implicit logic with explicit "because" / "however"),
                              UNEMBED multi-level embeddings, REDUCE-NOMINALIZATIONS

LEVEL 3 — conversational
  Reader profile: average educated newspaper-reading adult
  Structural-complexity tier:
    - Sentence-length (English): ≤25 words/sentence
    - Embedding depth: ≤2 (subordinate clauses with simple embedded relatives)
    - Suspension load: ≤2 (subordinate clauses mostly AFTER main verb;
                            short suspension acceptable)
    - Word order: canonical + common marked orders (topicalization, common inversions)
  Register/genre-tier: mainstream journalism, popular non-fiction, well-written conversational prose;
                       excludes nested suspension, center-embedding, archaic word orders, dense parentheticals
  Restructuring-test sketch: light — SPLIT only sentences > 25 words with dense embedding;
                              avoid suspension > 2 clauses;
                              keep linear subordination and simple relative clauses

LEVEL 4 — advanced
  Reader profile: university-educated reader / skilled non-native reading widely /
                  educated professional
  Structural-complexity tier:
    - Sentence-length (English): ~25–40 words/sentence (no strict upper bound; complexity is the constraint)
    - Embedding depth: ≤3 (nested subordination, parentheticals)
    - Suspension load: ≤3 (user's seed sentence is the anchor: 3 clauses
                            held before main verb resolves)
    - Word order: includes marked orders common in academic and literary register
    - Center-embedding: ≤1 (one center-embedded relative clause)
  Register/genre-tier: academic articles, contemporary literary fiction,
                       well-written essays, dense argumentative prose;
                       excludes 2-deep center-embedding, archaic word orders,
                       4+ clause suspension, specialist-domain syntactic conventions
  Restructuring-test sketch: minimal — avoid center-embedding 2+,
                              avoid 4+ clause suspension,
                              keep nested subordination and parentheticals,
                              keep marked academic/literary word orders

LEVEL 5 — native
  Reader profile: educated native reading broadly across literary registers
                  including historical / archaic / extreme-literary prose
  Structural-complexity tier:
    - Sentence-length (English): unlimited
    - Embedding depth: unlimited within general syntactic conventions
    - Suspension load: 4+ allowed (Henry James / Henry Adams style)
    - Word order: includes archaic, dialectal, KJV-Pauline inverted, Faulkner stream-of-consciousness
    - Center-embedding: ≤2 (Henry James / Henry Adams literary anchor)
  Register/genre-tier: literary-extreme prose, archaic-literary, dense Victorian,
                       KJV-Pauline, Faulkner stream-of-consciousness;
                       excludes only A2 specialist-domain syntactic conventions
                       (legal, mathematical, medical-research)
  Restructuring-test sketch: none for general syntax — preserve all general structures
                              including 2-deep center-embedding, archaic word orders,
                              4+ clause suspension.
                              A2 specialist-domain syntax substituted or footnoted
                              per the A1↔A2 boundary section.
```

**Key clarifications:**
- A1 syntactic-processing-capacity covers all GENERAL syntactic structures.

- Boundary test: "requires subject-domain training to parse" → A2; "requires only broad reading experience" → A1.
