**Question:** What is the adapted 4-component template for syntactic-processing-capacity (relative to vocabulary-breadth's template), what does each component cover, what sub-measures sit inside the structural-complexity tier, what restructuring actions are named, and why each adaptation?

**Verification criteria:**

- [ ] **4 components named:** (1) Reader profile with demographics + genre anchors; (2) Structural-complexity tier (umbrella with 5 sub-measures); (3) Register/genre-tier; (4) Restructuring-test sketch.

#### **P4 — Adjacent-level boundary specs**

**Question:** At each of the 4 adjacent-level transitions, what is the distinguishing principle and what sentence-pair examples illustrate the transition?

Sub-decomposes into 4 sibling pieces:

##### **P4.1 — `very_basic` ↔ `daily` boundary**

**Verification:**
- [ ] **Principle:** Shift from SVO-only simple sentences to coordinated sentences + simple relative clauses (≤1 embedded clause).
- [ ] **3–5 sentence pairs** (low-side `very_basic` ↔ high-side `daily`):
  - "The cat sat. The dog ran." ↔ "The cat sat on the mat, and the dog ran outside."
  - "The man was tired." ↔ "The man who came home was tired."
  - "I went home. I was hungry." ↔ "I went home because I was hungry."

##### **P4.2 — `daily` ↔ `conversational` boundary**

**Verification:**
- [ ] **Principle:** Shift from simple coordination + simple relative to MULTI-CLAUSE SUBORDINATION (linear; subordinate after or before main; embedding depth up to 2).
- [ ] **3–5 sentence pairs:**
  - "I went home because I was hungry." ↔ "When the cat sat down, the dog ran outside because it heard a noise that frightened it."
  - "The man who came home was tired." ↔ "The man, who had just come home from a long day, was tired but content."

##### **P4.3 — `conversational` ↔ `advanced` boundary**

**Verification:**
- [ ] **Principle:** Shift from linear multi-clause subordination to NESTED SUBORDINATION + PARENTHETICALS + SUSPENSION (up to 3 clauses suspended before main verb). The user's seed sentence is the anchor for the high side.
- [ ] **3–5 sentence pairs:**
  - "When the cat sat down, the dog ran outside because it heard a noise." ↔ "The cat's decision to sit, prompted by exhaustion that had built throughout the day, was met with relief by the dog who had been waiting outside."
  - "The argument succeeded." (conversational) ↔ "The argument, despite being couched in the kind of dense subordination that requires the reader to hold three clauses in working memory before encountering the main verb, succeeds." (advanced — user's seed)

##### **P4.4 — `advanced` ↔ `native` boundary**

**Verification:**
- [ ] **Principle:** Shift from nested subordination + 1-deep center-embedding to ALL GENERAL syntactic structures including 2-deep center-embedding, 4+ clause suspension, archaic word orders (KJV-Pauline inversion), Faulkner stream-of-consciousness.
- [ ] **3–5 sentence pairs:**
  - Advanced: "The argument, despite being couched in dense subordination, succeeds." (3-clause suspension; 1-deep)
  - Native: "Whilst it cannot be denied that the man, who, having decided that he ought, despite his misgivings, to attempt the journey, set forth at dawn and encountered numerous obstacles, was perhaps unprepared, his eventual success, though qualified, was sufficient to vindicate his decision." (4+ clause suspension; 2-deep center-embedding; archaic literary register)
  - Native (KJV-Pauline inversion): "Through Him to whom be glory, the work was done." (archaic inverted order)
  - Native (Faulkner-stream): no specific example; flag as a sub-style of native.
