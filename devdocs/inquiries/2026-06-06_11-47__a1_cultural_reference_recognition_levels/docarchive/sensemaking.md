## Phase 3 — Ambiguity Collapse

### Ambiguity A1: Canon-choice — configurable or assumed?
Should the cultural-reference-recognition level encode WHICH canon (Greek/Biblical/Confucian/Quranic) or assume a presumed-target canon?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** Bundle canon-choice into the level — different reader cultures have radically different canon-knowledge profiles; a "daily" Western reader and a "daily" Chinese reader catch very different references.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** Bundling conflates two ORTHOGONAL axes. A Western-secular reader can be `native` for Greek-Biblical canon AND `very_basic` for Quranic canon SIMULTANEOUSLY. Treating them as one axis collapses this information and forces a single-value-per-reader that can't reflect multi-canon literacy. The level framework must operate per-canon, with canon-choice as a separate audience-level config.

**Confidence:** HIGH — orthogonal-axis argument is structural.
**Resolution:** Canon-choice OUT OF SCOPE; level framework operates over a presumed-target canon. Canon-choice belongs at the audience level (an `audience.canon_set: list[str]` field, e.g., `["greek_roman", "biblical", "quranic", "said_nursi_corpus"]`).
**What is now fixed:** level operates per-canon; canon-set configured at audience level.
**What is no longer allowed:** encoding canon-choice as part of cultural-ref level.
**What now depends:** the audience-level field will need to specify canon-set; multi-canon texts may need per-canon reader-level configuration (future inquiry).
**Conceptual model change:** canon-choice now SEPARATE from canonicity-depth.

### Ambiguity A2: Whose canon — source vs target?
When source text references one canon (Islamic-Sufi for Nursi) but target reader has different canon (Western-secular), whose canon is the level relative to?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** Level should be source-canon-relative — captures how much of the source's reference set the reader catches.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** The reader's recognition is a property of the READER, not the source. A Western-secular reader's recognition of Quranic references is `very_basic` regardless of source text. The level captures what the reader brings TO the text, not what the text contains. Source-relative would be a different concept (source-canon-density), which is a TEXT property not a reader property — and a runtime input for action selection, not part of the reader-level definition.

**Confidence:** HIGH — anchored in receptive-only / reader-property argument.
**Resolution:** Level is TARGET-READER-RELATIVE. Captures what the reader recognizes; not what the text references.
**What is now fixed:** reader-level = reader's recognition depth.
**What is no longer allowed:** source-relative level interpretation.
**What now depends:** translator-AI uses level to decide handling based on reader's recognition; text's canon-density separately observed by AI from source text.
**Conceptual model change:** explicit canon-relativity = reader-side.

### Ambiguity A3: Markedness — level dimension or action-modifier?
Is the reference's markedness (signposted vs unsignposted) part of the level definition or a separate axis affecting handling action choice?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** Markedness is a level dimension; readers vary in their ability to catch UNSIGNPOSTED references (advanced can; very_basic can't).

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** Markedness is a TEXT property (the source text either signposts or doesn't). Reader-recognition-depth is orthogonal — an advanced reader can catch unsignposted references AND signposted ones; a very_basic reader misses unsignposted ones AND struggles with signposted if they don't know the canon. The level captures recognition-depth; markedness modulates HOW EASILY the reader can catch a reference at their level — but it's the TEXT's property, not the reader's.

**Confidence:** HIGH — text-property vs reader-property structural distinction.
**Resolution:** Markedness = text-property; orthogonal to reader-level. Markedness modulates ACTION choice (unmarked reference at very_basic → FOOTNOTE; marked reference at very_basic → INLINE-GLOSS the content while preserving the marker).
**What is now fixed:** markedness = text-property; not a level dimension.
**What is no longer allowed:** bundling markedness into reader-level.
**What now depends:** action-selection logic uses markedness as runtime input.
**Conceptual model change:** markedness explicitly assigned to text-side; reader-level dimensionally smaller and cleaner.

**What is no longer allowed:** bundling transparency into reader-level.

**Conceptual model change:** transparency explicitly assigned to reference-side.

### Ambiguity A6: Canonicity tiers — 5 to match levels or independent?
Should canonicity tiers match reader-level count (5 ↔ 5) or be independent?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** Canonicity has its own natural breakdown that may not be 5; force-mapping to reader levels is artificial.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** The 5 canonicity tiers (ubiquitous / educated-mainstream / literary-educated / specialist-canonical / scholar-canonical) come from Hirsch-style cultural literacy research and Bourdieu's cultural-capital stratification. These are not arbitrary — they correspond to plausible recognition-depth strata in the cultural-literacy literature. The 5-tier breakdown coincidentally matches 5 reader levels because both follow a natural stratification of cultural-knowledge depth.

**Confidence:** HIGH — anchored in cultural-literacy research.
**Resolution:** 5 canonicity tiers map 1-to-1 to 5 reader levels:
- `very_basic` recognizes ubiquitous-canon (and unreliably — misses subtle ones)
- `daily` recognizes ubiquitous + parts of educated-mainstream
- `conversational` recognizes ubiquitous + educated-mainstream (complete)
- `advanced` recognizes ubiquitous + educated-mainstream + literary-educated
- `native` recognizes all 5 tiers including specialist-canonical + scholar-canonical
**What is now fixed:** 5-tier canonicity ladder maps to 5 reader levels.
**What is no longer allowed:** independent canonicity-tier count.
**What now depends:** per-level prose specifies which canonicity tiers fire which action; tier definitions referenceable across cultural canons.
**Conceptual model change:** clean 1-to-1 tier-to-level mapping.
