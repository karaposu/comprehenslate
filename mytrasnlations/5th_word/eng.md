If you wish to see how truly the performance of the prayer (*namaz*) and the avoidance of the major sins (*kebâir*) is a duty proper to the human being — and how innate, how fitting an outcome it is of humanity's very making — then look at, and listen to, this little parable.

During a wartime mobilization, two soldiers found themselves together in one battalion. The one was trained, and devoted to his duty; the other raw, and devoted to coddling himself (a *nefisperver*, one who indulges the *nefs* — the lower, appetitive self). The duty-devoted soldier attended to his drill and to combat (*cihâd*); to his rations and provisions he gave no thought at all. For he had understood this: that to feed him, to equip him, to nurse him when sick — even, in case of need, to place the very morsel in his mouth — all of that is the state's business. His own real business is the drill and the combat. Even so, in matters of provisions and equipment, he would on occasion lend a hand. He tends the cauldron, scrubs the mess-tin, fetches and carries. If you ask him, *What are you doing?*, he says: *I am performing the state's corvée.* He does not say: *I am working for my keep.*

The other soldier — the belly-serving raw one — paid no heed to the drill or to the battle. "That is the state's affair. What is it to me?" he would say. Forever thinking of his keep, he chased after it; he abandoned his battalion, slipped off to the marketplace, and busied himself with buying and selling. One day, his trained comrade said to him:

— Brother, your real duty is the drill and the fighting. You were brought here for *that*. Place your trust in the Padishah. He will not leave you hungry. That is *his* business. Besides, you are weak, you are poor; you cannot have yourself fed wherever you please. Besides, this is a time of struggle and mobilization. Besides, they will brand you a mutineer (*âsî*) and inflict the punishment. Yes — two duties stand before us. The one is the Padishah's duty; from time to time we shoulder a portion of his corvée — and feeding us is his task. The other is our own duty; in it the Padishah aids us with every facility — and that duty is the drill and the war. Now then: if that wayward soldier closes his ear to the trained warrior-comrade, you can well understand the depth of the danger in which he stands!

Behold, O my lazy soul! That wave-tossed field of battle is this turbulent worldly life. That army apportioned out into battalions is human society. And that battalion is the Muslim community (*cemâ'at-ı İslâmiye*) of this present age. As for the two soldiers — the one is the God-conscious Muslim (*müttakî müslüman*) who knows and discharges his religious obligations (*ferâiz*), and who, that he might forsake the major sins and commit no sin at all, wages inner struggle (*mücâhede*) against the *nefs* and against Satan. The other is the ruined transgressor (*fâsık-ı hâsir*) who, plunged into livelihood-anxiety to the very degree of impugning the True Provider (*Rezzâk-ı Hakîkî* — He alone who gives and sustains every life), abandons his obligations and commits whatever sin he meets along the road of his earning. And that drill and that instruction is — at its head, the prayer — worship (*ibâdet*). And that war is: to wage struggle against the *nefs*, against passion (*hevâ*), against the devils of *jinn* and of men — and so to save one's heart and one's spirit from sins, from base traits of character, and from eternal ruin. And those two duties are: the one — to give life and to feed it. The other — to worship the One who gives life and feeds it, to plead before Him; to entrust oneself to Him (*tevekkül*), and to rest secure in Him.

Yes — that life which is the most brilliant miracle of the *Samad*'s artistry (*Samad* — the Eternally Self-Sufficient upon whom all things turn) and a marvel of Lordly wisdom — whosoever has given it and made it, that same One feeds and sustains it with sustenance (*rızk*). It can be no other. Do you want a proof? The feeblest, most foolish animal is the best fed — as with the worms inside the fruit and the fish in the sea. The most helpless, most delicate creature receives the finest provision — as with infants, and the young of every species.

Yes — to grasp that the channel of lawful sustenance does not come by way of power and free choice, but rather by way of helplessness and weakness, it is enough to weigh fish against fox, suckling against beast of prey, tree against animal. It follows, then: he who abandons his prayer out of livelihood-anxiety resembles that soldier who quit his drill and his trench-post to beg in the marketplace. But the one who, after he has performed his prayer, goes himself, in person, to seek his rations from the Kitchen of Mercy (*matbaha-i rahmet*) of the August and All-Generous Provider — that he may not become a burden upon others — his going is fair, it is manly, and it too is a form of worship.

Moreover — that the human being was created for worship is shown by his very nature (*fıtrat*) and by the inward spiritual apparatus given him. For in respect of the labor and capability required for his worldly life, he does not reach the rank of the lowliest sparrow. Yet in respect of the knowledge, the avowed neediness, the humble entreaty, and the worship required for his spiritual and otherworldly life, he stands as the sultan and commander of the whole animal kingdom.

Therefore, O my soul! — if you make worldly life your ultimate aim and labor for it without rest, you become as a single foot-soldier in the ranks of the lowliest sparrow. But if you make the life of the hereafter your ultimate aim, and make this present life a means and a tilth (*mezra'a* — a field of sowing) for it, and labor accordingly — then in that hour you become as a great commander of the animal kingdom, and in this world a cherished and supplicating servant (*'abd*) of Almighty God, an honored and esteemed guest of His.

Here, then, are the two paths before you. Choose whichever you will. And ask guidance and divine enabling (*hidâyet ve tevfîk*) from the Most Merciful of the Merciful (*Erhamü'r-Râhimîn*)..
